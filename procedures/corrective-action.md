## Purpose

This procedure establishes a systematic approach to:
- React to nonconformities promptly
- Analyze causes of nonconformities
- Take action to eliminate root causes
- Prevent recurrence
- Continuously improve QMS effectiveness
- Learn from problems and share knowledge

**ISO 9001:2026 Alignment**: Enhanced emphasis on data-driven analysis, digital tracking, and integration with risk management and improvement cycles.

---

## Scope

This procedure applies to nonconformities arising from:
- **Product/Service**: Not meeting specifications or customer requirements
- **Process**: Process failure or inadequate performance
- **Customer Feedback**: Complaints, returns, dissatisfaction
- **Internal Audits**: Audit findings and observations
- **External Audits**: Certification, customer, regulatory audits
- **Supplier Issues**: Nonconforming materials, late deliveries
- **Risk Materialization**: Identified risks that occurred
- **Employee Reports**: Safety issues, quality concerns
- **Data Analysis**: Trends indicating systemic issues

**Types of Actions**:
- **Correction**: Immediate fix to eliminate the nonconformity
- **Corrective Action**: Action to eliminate the cause and prevent recurrence
- **Preventive Action**: Action to eliminate the cause of a potential nonconformity (ISO 9001:2026 integrates this into risk-based thinking)

---

## Definitions

### Nonconformity (NC)
Non-fulfillment of a requirement. Can relate to product, process, system.

### Correction
Action to eliminate a detected nonconformity. Immediate fix of the problem.
- Example: Rework a defective part, correct an error in a document

### Corrective Action (CA)
Action to eliminate the cause of a nonconformity to prevent recurrence. Addresses the root cause.
- Example: Improve training program, redesign process step, update procedure

### Root Cause
Fundamental reason for the nonconformity; if eliminated, the NC will not recur.

### CAPA
Corrective and Preventive Action (combined term often used in quality systems)

### Preventive Action
Action to eliminate the cause of a potential nonconformity. (Note: ISO 9001:2026 addresses this through risk-based thinking rather than a separate preventive action process)

### Effectiveness Verification
Confirmation that corrective actions achieved the intended result and the nonconformity has not recurred.

### Recurrence
Same or similar nonconformity happening again after corrective action.

### Trend
Pattern of repeated nonconformities indicating a systemic issue.

## Responsibilities

### All Personnel
- Report nonconformities immediately
- Participate in root cause analysis
- Implement assigned corrective actions
- Follow updated procedures

### Process Owners / Supervisors
- Implement immediate corrections
- Initiate corrective action requests
- Lead root cause analysis
- Implement corrective actions
- Verify effectiveness

### Quality Manager
- Manage CAPA system
- Facilitate root cause analysis
- Prioritize corrective actions
- Monitor CAPA progress
- Report CAPA metrics to management
- Ensure actions are effective
- Maintain CAPA records

### Top Management
- Provide resources for corrective actions
- Review significant nonconformities
- Ensure systemic issues are addressed
- Support culture of problem-solving

### CAPA Review Board (for significant issues)
- Management representative
- Quality Manager
- Affected process owners
- Technical experts (as needed)
- Reviews complex/high-impact CAPAs
- Makes resource allocation decisions

### Detailed Process Steps

---

### D0: Plan and Prepare

**Objective**: Quickly assess the situation and organize response

**Activities**:

1. **Identify the Nonconformity**
   - Source: Inspection, audit, complaint, incident, data analysis
   - Document what was discovered

2. **Assess Severity and Urgency**

   | Level | Criteria | Response Time |
   |-------|----------|---------------|
   | **CRITICAL** | Safety risk, regulatory violation, major customer impact | Immediate (same day) |
   | **HIGH** | Significant customer impact, financial loss, systemic issue | Within 24 hours |
   | **MEDIUM** | Moderate impact, affects quality | Within 1 week |
   | **LOW** | Minor issue, isolated incident | Within 2 weeks |

3. **Assign Ownership**
   - Process owner is typically the CAPA owner
   - Quality Manager for cross-functional issues

4. **Determine if Team Needed**
   - Simple issues: Individual owner
   - Complex issues: Cross-functional team

5. **Open CAPA Record**
   - Assign unique CAPA number
   - Enter into CAPA tracking system

---

### D1: Describe the Problem

**Objective**: Clearly define what the problem is (not why it happened yet)

**Tools**: 5W2H (Who, What, Where, When, Why, How, How Much)

**Problem Statement Template**:

```
PROBLEM DESCRIPTION

WHAT is the problem?
[Specific description of the nonconformity]

WHERE was it detected/occurred?
[Location, process step, department]

WHEN was it detected? When did it occur?
[Date and time, frequency]

WHO is affected? Who detected it?
[Customers, employees, departments]

HOW was it detected?
[Inspection, test, complaint, audit]

HOW MUCH / HOW MANY?
[Quantity affected, magnitude of impact]

(WHY will be addressed in root cause analysis)

Example:
"On January 15, 2025, 50 units of Product X were found to have 
dimension Y out of specification (4.95mm vs. 5.00mm ± 0.02mm) 
during final inspection. The defect was detected by Inspector A 
using calibrated calipers. These units were from production batch 
B-2025-015 manufactured on Line 3 during the day shift. Customer 
shipment was scheduled for January 16."
```

**Good Problem Statement Characteristics**:
- Specific and factual
- Quantified (numbers, dates)
- Objective (no blame)
- Focused (one problem at a time)

---

### D2: Implement Containment (Immediate Correction)

**Objective**: Stop the problem from affecting customers or spreading

**Containment Actions**:

1. **Isolate Nonconforming Product/Output**
   - Quarantine affected items
   - Tag or mark clearly (e.g., "HOLD - Do Not Use")
   - Document quantity and location

2. **Disposition Decision** (per Clause 8.7)
   - **Correction**: Rework to meet requirements
   - **Scrap**: Dispose of unusable items
   - **Use-As-Is**: Accept with concession (requires approval)
   - **Regrading**: Downgrade to lower specification
   - **Return to Supplier**: If externally provided

3. **Protect the Customer**
   - Inspect inventory and work-in-progress
   - Check if any shipped to customer (recall if needed)
   - Notify customer if affected product delivered
   - Sort suspect lots (100% inspection if necessary)

4. **Stop Production if Needed**
   - If process is producing nonconforming output
   - Until cause is understood and corrected

5. **Document Containment**
   - What actions were taken
   - Quantity affected
   - Disposition of nonconforming items
   - Customer notifications

**Correction ≠ Corrective Action**:
- Correction fixes THIS instance
- Corrective Action prevents FUTURE instances

---

### D3: Identify Root Cause

**Objective**: Determine why the nonconformity occurred (root cause, not symptoms)

**Root Cause Analysis (RCA) Process**:

1. **Gather Data and Evidence**
   - Review records, logs, data
   - Interview personnel involved
   - Inspect equipment, materials, conditions
   - Recreate conditions if possible

2. **Use RCA Tools** (See section below for detailed methods)
   - 5 Whys
   - Fishbone Diagram (Ishikawa)
   - Fault Tree Analysis
   - Pareto Analysis
   - Process Mapping

3. **Verify Root Cause**
   - Test hypothesis: "If we eliminate this cause, will the problem be prevented?"
   - Look for evidence that confirms root cause
   - Distinguish root cause from contributing factors

**Root Cause vs. Symptom**:

| Symptom (Surface) | Root Cause (Underlying) |
|-------------------|-------------------------|
| Part is out of spec | Machine calibration drifted because maintenance procedure doesn't specify calibration frequency |
| Employee made error | Training program doesn't include hands-on practice or competency verification |
| Supplier delivered late | No backup supplier identified; single-source risk not mitigated |
| Document was outdated | No periodic review process; no notification system for document updates |

**Root Cause Categories** (Fishbone):
- **Man/People**: Training, competence, fatigue, communication
- **Method/Process**: Procedures, work instructions, process design
- **Machine/Equipment**: Malfunction, calibration, maintenance, capacity
- **Material**: Supplier quality, specification, handling, storage
- **Measurement**: Inspection method, gage accuracy, sampling plan
- **Environment**: Temperature, humidity, lighting, workspace layout
- **Management**: Resources, priorities, culture, oversight

---

### D4: Choose Corrective Actions

**Objective**: Select actions that will eliminate the root cause and prevent recurrence

**Action Development**:

1. **Brainstorm Solutions**
   - Team generates multiple options
   - Don't evaluate initially (encourage creativity)
   - Consider multiple levels of action

2. **Evaluate Options**

   | Criteria | Consideration |
   |----------|---------------|
   | **Effectiveness** | Will it truly eliminate root cause? |
   | **Feasibility** | Can we implement it with available resources? |
   | **Cost** | What is the investment vs. benefit? |
   | **Time** | How quickly can it be implemented? |
   | **Sustainability** | Will it be maintained long-term? |
   | **Side Effects** | Could it cause other problems? |

3. **Select Best Solution**
   - May be single action or combination
   - Balance quick wins with long-term fixes
   - Get stakeholder buy-in

**Types of Corrective Actions**:

| Action Type | Example |
|-------------|---------|
| **Procedure Update** | Revise work instruction to include missing step |
| **Training** | Provide additional training and verify competency |
| **Process Redesign** | Change process flow to eliminate error opportunity (poka-yoke) |
| **Equipment** | Repair, replace, upgrade, or add equipment |
| **Material/Supplier** | Change supplier, add inspection, update specification |
| **Resource** | Add personnel, tools, or time |
| **Design Change** | Modify product/service design to prevent issue |
| **System Improvement** | Implement new control, checklist, or monitoring |

**Hierarchy of Controls** (most to least effective):
1. **Elimination**: Remove the hazard/cause entirely
2. **Substitution**: Replace with less risky alternative
3. **Engineering Controls**: Redesign process/equipment (poka-yoke/mistake-proofing)
4. **Administrative Controls**: Procedures, training, warnings
5. **PPE / Monitoring**: Personal protection, oversight (least effective)

---

### D5: Verify Actions Work (Test)

**Objective**: Confirm the corrective action is effective before full implementation

**Verification Methods**:

1. **Pilot Test**
   - Implement on small scale
   - Monitor closely
   - Collect data on effectiveness

2. **Simulation**
   - Test in controlled environment
   - Use samples or mockups

3. **Review by Experts**
   - Technical review
   - Peer review

4. **Data Analysis**
   - Compare before and after data
   - Statistical analysis if applicable

**Success Criteria**:
- Root cause is addressed
- Nonconformity does not recur
- No negative side effects
- Process performance improved

**If Verification Fails**:
- Return to D3 (root cause may be incorrect)
- Return to D4 (corrective action insufficient)
- Revise and re-verify

---

### D6: Implement Permanently

**Objective**: Roll out corrective action fully and sustain it

**Implementation Steps**:

1. **Update Documentation**
   - Revise procedures, work instructions, forms
   - Follow document control procedure (PRO-003)
   - Ensure changes are approved

2. **Communicate Changes**
   - Announce to affected personnel
   - Explain why change is being made
   - Provide timeline

3. **Train Personnel**
   - Conduct training on new method
   - Verify understanding
   - Document training records

4. **Provide Resources**
   - Ensure tools, equipment, materials available
   - Budget allocation if needed

5. **Update Risk Register**
   - Reflect new controls in risk management
   - Adjust risk ratings if applicable

6. **Implementation Date**
   - Set effective date for change
   - Monitor during transition

---

### D7: Prevent Recurrence (Systematic Prevention)

**Objective**: Apply learnings to prevent similar issues elsewhere

**Horizontal Deployment**:

1. **Identify Similar Processes**
   - Where else could this problem occur?
   - Other products, lines, locations, systems

2. **Apply Preventive Measures**
   - Implement similar controls in similar areas
   - Example: If training gap found for Process A, review training for all processes

3. **Update Standards**
   - Incorporate learnings into organizational standards
   - Update templates, checklists, guidelines

4. **Improve Systems**
   - Enhance QMS processes
   - Example: If document control issue, strengthen document review process

**Knowledge Sharing**:

5. **Lessons Learned Documentation**
   - Create case study
   - Share in team meetings, newsletters, training
   - Add to knowledge base

6. **Continuous Improvement**
   - Feed insights into improvement initiatives
   - Update risk assessments
   - Enhance preventive maintenance

---

### D8: Recognize Team and Close CAPA

**Objective**: Acknowledge contributions and formally close

**Closure Activities**:

1. **Verify Effectiveness** (detailed in next section)
   - Confirm nonconformity has not recurred
   - Controls are working
   - Performance metrics improved

2. **Final Documentation**
   - Complete CAPA record
   - Attach all evidence
   - Summarize results

3. **Recognize Contributions**
   - Thank team members
   - Celebrate success
   - Share positive outcomes

4. **Approve Closure**
   - Quality Manager reviews
   - Confirms all steps completed
   - Approves closure in system

5. **Archive**
   - Retain CAPA record per retention schedule
   - Available for reference, audits

## Root Cause Analysis Methods

### Method 1: 5 Whys

**Description**: Ask "Why?" repeatedly (typically 5 times) to drill down from symptom to root cause

**Process**:
1. State the problem
2. Ask "Why did this happen?"
3. For each answer, ask "Why?" again
4. Continue until root cause is reached (you can't ask why anymore, or answer is actionable)

**Example**:

```
Problem: Customer received late delivery

Why #1: Why was delivery late?
→ Shipment left warehouse 2 days late

Why #2: Why did shipment leave warehouse late?
→ Product was not ready on scheduled date

Why #3: Why was product not ready?
→ Production was delayed

Why #4: Why was production delayed?
→ Machine broke down and was out of service for 3 days

Why #5: Why did machine break down?
→ Preventive maintenance was not performed on schedule

ROOT CAUSE: Preventive maintenance schedule is not being followed
→ CORRECTIVE ACTION: Implement automated maintenance reminders 
   and accountability system
```

**Advantages**:
- Simple, quick
- No special tools needed
- Effective for simple problems

**Limitations**:
- May oversimplify complex issues
- Multiple branches not easily captured
- Relies on asking the right questions

---

### Method 2: Fishbone Diagram (Ishikawa / Cause-and-Effect Diagram)

**Description**: Visual tool showing potential causes organized by categories

**Process**:
1. Draw fish skeleton with problem as the "head"
2. Draw major bones representing categories (6Ms typically)
3. Brainstorm causes for each category
4. Drill down to sub-causes
5. Identify most likely root causes to investigate

**Diagram Structure**:

```
         Man           Method          Machine
           \             |             /
            \            |            /
             \           |           /
              \          |          /
               \         |         /
                \        |        /
                 \       |       /
                  \      |      /
                   \     |     /
                    \    |    /
                     \   |   /
                      \  |  /
                       \ | /
                        \|/
                    ═══════════► PROBLEM
                        /|\
                       / | \
                      /  |  \
                     /   |   \
                    /    |    \
                   /     |     \
                  /      |      \
                 /       |       \
                /        |        \
               /         |         \
              /          |          \
             /           |           \
            /            |            \
           /             |             \
      Material       Measurement   Environment
```

**Categories (6Ms)**:
- **Man/People**: Human factors
- **Method**: Processes and procedures
- **Machine**: Equipment and tools
- **Material**: Raw materials, consumables
- **Measurement**: Inspection, testing, data
- **Environment**: Physical conditions, context

*Can add:* Management, Mother Nature (natural causes)

**Example**:

```
Problem: High defect rate in product assembly

MAN                          METHOD
• Insufficient training      • Unclear work instruction
• New employees             • No quality checks
• Fatigue                   • Process variation

MACHINE                      MATERIAL
• Worn tooling              • Supplier quality variation
• No calibration            • Wrong material grade
• Old equipment             • Storage conditions

MEASUREMENT                  ENVIRONMENT
• Inspection sampling       • Poor lighting
• Gage not calibrated       • Temperature fluctuation
• Subjective criteria       • Cramped workspace
```

**Advantages**:
- Visual and comprehensive
- Organizes many potential causes
- Team brainstorming tool
- Identifies multiple contributing factors

**Limitations**:
- Doesn't identify THE root cause (requires further investigation)
- Can become overwhelming with too many branches

---

### Method 3: Pareto Analysis (80/20 Rule)

**Description**: Identify the vital few causes responsible for the majority of problems

**Process**:
1. Collect data on problem types/causes
2. Categorize and count frequency
3. Sort from most to least frequent
4. Calculate cumulative percentage
5. Create bar chart with cumulative line
6. Focus on top causes (typically 20% of causes = 80% of problems)

**Example Data**:

| Defect Type | Count | % | Cumulative % |
|-------------|-------|---|--------------|
| Missing parts | 45 | 45% | 45% |
| Wrong assembly | 30 | 30% | 75% |
| Damage | 15 | 15% | 90% |
| Wrong part | 7 | 7% | 97% |
| Other | 3 | 3% | 100% |
| **TOTAL** | **100** | **100%** | |

**Pareto Chart**: Bar chart with bars descending + cumulative line

**Interpretation**: Focus CAPA efforts on Missing Parts and Wrong Assembly (75% of defects)

**Advantages**:
- Data-driven prioritization
- Focuses resources on biggest impact
- Visual and easy to understand

**Limitations**:
- Requires data collection
- May miss low-frequency but high-impact issues

---

### Method 4: Process Mapping / Flowcharting

**Description**: Visual representation of process steps to identify where problem occurs

**Process**:
1. Map the current process (as-is)
2. Identify where nonconformity occurred
3. Analyze that step and preceding steps
4. Look for missing controls, decision points, inputs
5. Design improved process (to-be)

**Symbols**:
- **Oval**: Start/End
- **Rectangle**: Process step
- **Diamond**: Decision point
- **Arrow**: Flow direction
- **Cylinder**: Database/storage
- **Parallelogram**: Input/Output

**Use For**:
- Understanding complex processes
- Identifying process gaps
- Finding rework loops
- Spotting missing controls

---

### Method 5: Is/Is Not Analysis (Kepner-Tregoe)

**Description**: Compare where problem exists vs. doesn't exist to narrow down cause

**Matrix**:

| Dimension | IS (Problem exists) | IS NOT (Problem absent) | Distinction |
|-----------|---------------------|-------------------------|-------------|
| **What** | Specific defect type | Other defect types | What is unique about this defect? |
| **Where** | Line 3, Station 2 | Lines 1, 2, 4 | What's different about Line 3? |
| **When** | Day shift, Monday AM | Other shifts/times | What's unique about Monday AM? |
| **Extent** | 45 out of 100 units | Remaining 55 units OK | What's different about the 45? |
| **Who** | Operator A | Operators B, C | What's unique about Operator A? |

**Analysis**: Look for patterns in the distinctions → leads to root cause

**Example**:
- Problem only on Line 3 → Investigate Line 3 equipment
- Problem only with Operator A → Training or technique issue
- Problem only on Monday AM → Weekend shutdown process or startup procedure

## Preventive Actions

**ISO 9001:2026 Approach**: Preventive action is integrated into risk-based thinking (Clause 6.1) rather than a separate process.

**Preventive Opportunities**:

1. **Proactive Risk Management**
   - Identify potential nonconformities in risk assessment
   - Implement controls before problems occur
   - See PRO-001: Risk Management

2. **Trend Analysis**
   - Monitor data for early warning signs
   - Act on trends before they become problems
   - Example: Increasing complaint rate, decreasing yield

3. **Near-Miss Reporting**
   - Encourage reporting of "close calls"
   - Treat as learning opportunities
   - Implement preventive measures

4. **Benchmarking**
   - Learn from others' experiences
   - Industry best practices
   - Lessons learned databases

5. **Proactive Process Improvement**
   - Continuous improvement initiatives
   - Kaizen events
   - Lean Six Sigma projects

**When to Open a CAPA for Prevention**:
- Trend data shows increasing issues
- Near-miss with potential for serious consequence
- Risk assessment identifies significant risk
- Benchmark reveals gap
- Audit observation suggests future problem

### Verification Methods

| Method | Description | When to Use |
|--------|-------------|-------------|
| **Data Analysis** | Compare performance metrics before/after | Process changes, systemic issues |
| **Observation** | Watch process in operation | Procedure updates, behavioral changes |
| **Record Review** | Review quality records over time | Documentation improvements |
| **Audit** | Internal audit of corrected area | Significant system changes |
| **Customer Feedback** | Monitor complaints/satisfaction | Customer-facing issues |
| **Testing** | Re-create original condition | Technical/design changes |

### Verification Timing

**Risk-Based Approach**:

| CAPA Severity | Verification Timeframe | Rationale |
|---------------|------------------------|-----------|
| **CRITICAL** | 30 days + ongoing monitoring | Must confirm quickly due to high risk |
| **HIGH** | 60 days | Reasonable time for pattern to emerge |
| **MEDIUM** | 90 days | Standard timeframe |
| **LOW** | 90-180 days | Longer observation acceptable |

### Success Criteria Examples

**Good Criteria** (SMART):
- "Zero recurrence of nonconformity for 90 days"
- "Defect rate reduced from 5% to <2% and sustained for 2 months"
- "100% of personnel trained and competency verified"
- "Procedure followed correctly in 10 consecutive observations"
- "Customer complaints on this issue reduced by 80%"

**Poor Criteria**:
- "Problem is fixed" (too vague)

## Digital CAPA Management

**ISO 9001:2026 Enhancement**: Leverage digital systems for more effective CAPA management

### Digital CAPA System Features

#### 1. Automated Workflow
- **Auto-Assignment**: Route CAPA to responsible person
- **Notifications**: Email/SMS alerts for due dates
- **Escalation**: Auto-escalate overdue CAPAs
- **Approvals**: Electronic approval workflow

#### 2. Centralized Tracking
- **Dashboard**: Real-time CAPA status
- **Searchable Database**: Find similar past CAPAs
- **Document Linking**: Attach photos, reports, evidence
- **Version History**: Track all changes

#### 3. Data Analytics
- **Trend Analysis**: Identify patterns
- **Pareto Charts**: Auto-generate from data
- **Metrics**: Cycle time, recurrence rate, effectiveness
- **Predictive Analytics**: AI identifies emerging issues

#### 4. Integration
- **Link to Audits**: Auto-create CAPA from audit findings
- **Link to Complaints**: Auto-create from complaint system
- **Link to Risk Register**: Update risks when CAPA closed
- **Link to Training**: Trigger training when procedure updated

#### 5. Mobile Capability
- Report issues from anywhere (shop floor, field)
- Photo evidence upload
- Real-time updates
- Notifications

### Digital RCA Tools

- **Online Fishbone**: Collaborative digital diagrams
- **5 Whys App**: Guided questioning
- **Data Visualization**: Automatic Pareto, control charts
- **Process Mapping Software**: Digital flowcharts

### Benefits of Digital CAPA

## Performance Metrics

### CAPA Metrics to Monitor

| Metric | Description | Target |
|--------|-------------|--------|
| **Open CAPAs** | Number currently open | Trend decreasing |
| **Overdue CAPAs** | Past due date | <10% of total |
| **Cycle Time** | Days from open to close | <60 days (medium) |
| **Recurrence Rate** | % of closed CAPAs that recur | <5% |
| **Effectiveness Rate** | % verified effective | >95% |
| **On-Time Closure** | % closed by due date | >85% |
| **Time to Containment** | Hours from detection to containment | <24 hours (high severity) |
| **Root Cause Identified** | % with documented RCA | 100% |
| **Source Analysis** | Where CAPAs originate (complaints, audits, etc.) | Track for trends |

### Reporting

**Monthly CAPA Report**:
- Open/Closed/Overdue summary
- Aging analysis (how long open)
- Top categories (Pareto of root causes)
- Recurrence tracking
- Resource needs

**Management Review Input**:
- CAPA trends
- Systemic issues requiring attention
- Effectiveness of corrective action process
- Resource allocation needs
