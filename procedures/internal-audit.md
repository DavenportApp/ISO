## Purpose

This procedure establishes a systematic approach to:
- Verify QMS implementation and effectiveness
- Ensure compliance with ISO 9001:2026 requirements
- Evaluate process performance against objectives
- Identify improvement opportunities
- Provide assurance to management and stakeholders
- Drive continual improvement

**ISO 9001:2026 Alignment**: Incorporates enhanced requirements for digital auditing, climate considerations, risk-based audit planning, and stakeholder-focused audits.

---

## Scope

This procedure applies to:
- All QMS processes and functions
- All organizational locations and departments
- External providers (where applicable and agreed)
- Digital systems and data management
- Climate and sustainability programs
- Integrated management systems (if applicable)

**Exclusions**: Financial audits (unless related to QMS), HR audits (unless QMS-related)

## Definitions

### Internal Audit
Systematic, independent, and documented process for obtaining audit evidence and evaluating it objectively.

### Audit Program
Set of one or more audits planned for a specific timeframe and directed towards a specific purpose.

### Audit Plan
Description of activities and arrangements for a specific audit.

### Audit Criteria
Set of requirements used as a reference against which audit evidence is compared (ISO 9001:2026, procedures, objectives).

### Audit Evidence
Records, statements of fact, or other information relevant and verifiable (documents, observations, interviews).

### Audit Finding
Result of evaluation of audit evidence against audit criteria. Can be conformity, nonconformity, or observation.

### Nonconformity
Non-fulfillment of a requirement.

### Observation
Notable practice or potential improvement area (not a nonconformity).

### Audit Conclusion
Outcome of an audit after considering audit objectives and all findings.

### Risk-Based Audit
Audit approach that focuses resources on higher-risk areas.

### Risk-Based Audit Planning Criteria

| **Complexity** | Complex, critical processes | Simple, routine processes |
| **Changes** | Recent significant changes | Stable, unchanged processes |
| **Customer Impact** | Direct customer impact | Indirect support processes |
| **Regulatory** | Heavily regulated | Minimal regulations |
| **Previous Audit** | Long time since last audit | Recently audited |
| **KPIs** | Poor performance indicators | Meeting/exceeding targets |
| **Complaints** | Customer or employee complaints | No complaints |

## Audit Planning

### Planning Timeline

**Notification**: Minimum 2 weeks before audit (may be shorter for follow-up audits)

**Planning Activities**:

#### 1. Define Audit Objectives

Examples:
- Verify conformity to ISO 9001:2026 Clause X
- Evaluate effectiveness of corrective actions from previous audit
- Assess implementation of new process
- Verify risk controls are working
- Evaluate digital system data integrity

#### 2. Determine Audit Scope

Define:
- **Processes**: Which processes will be audited
- **Locations**: Physical and virtual locations
- **Time Period**: Records to be reviewed (e.g., last 3 months)
- **Standards**: ISO 9001:2026 clauses, procedures, work instructions
- **Exclusions**: What is NOT included

#### 3. Review Background Information

- Previous audit reports and findings
- Nonconformities and corrective actions
- Process documentation (procedures, work instructions)
- Risk register entries
- Performance data and KPIs
- Customer complaints
- Organizational changes

#### 4. Assign Audit Team

**Independence Requirements**:
- Auditors must NOT audit their own work
- Must be objective and impartial
- No direct responsibility for audited area

**Team Roles**:
- **Lead Auditor**: Overall audit management
- **Auditors**: Specific process/area audits
- **Technical Experts**: Subject matter expertise (optional)
- **Observers/Trainees**: For development (optional)

**Audit Plan Contents**:

• Auditees: [Department/Process Owners]

#### 6. Prepare Audit Checklist

**Checklist Development**:
- Based on audit criteria (ISO clauses, procedures)
- Mix of closed and open-ended questions
- Focus on key requirements and risks
- Include performance review questions

| ISO Clause | Requirement | Audit Questions |
|------------|-------------|-----------------|
| 4.4 | QMS Processes | What are the inputs and outputs of this process? How is sequence and interaction determined? |

| 7.2 | Competence | What competencies are required? How is competence verified? |
| 8.5.1 | Production Control | How do you ensure controlled conditions? Show me evidence. |
| 9.1.3 | Analysis & Evaluation | What data do you analyze? How do you use results? |
| 10.2 | Corrective Action | Show me a recent corrective action. Was root cause analyzed? |

**ISO 9001:2026 Enhanced Areas**:

### Opening Meeting

**Attendees**: Audit team, auditees, process owners, management representative

**Agenda**:
1. Introductions
2. Confirm audit objectives, scope, and criteria
3. Review audit schedule and methods
4. Confirm logistics (access, guides, confidentiality)
5. Clarify roles and responsibilities
6. Questions and concerns

**Lead Auditor Emphasizes**:
- Audit is to help improve, not to blame
- Objective and evidence-based approach
- Auditee cooperation appreciated
- Findings will be discussed before closing meeting

### Information Gathering Techniques

#### A. Document Review

**Purpose**: Verify documented information meets requirements

**What to Review**:
- Procedures, work instructions, specifications
- Records (completed forms, logs, reports)
- Training records
- Calibration records
- Risk assessments
- Performance data

**Questions to Ask**:
- Is documentation current and approved?
- Is it accessible to those who need it?
- Is it being followed in practice?

#### B. Observations

**Purpose**: Verify activities are performed as documented

**What to Observe**:
- Work being performed
- Equipment operation and conditions
- Environmental conditions
- Safety practices
- Display of quality information

**Technique**:
- Don't interfere with work
- Watch full process cycles when possible
- Note what you see, not what you think

#### C. Interviews

**Purpose**: Gather information from personnel

**Interview Techniques**:
- **Open-ended questions**: "How do you...?", "What do you do if...?"
- **Active listening**: Let them speak, don't interrupt
- **Non-threatening**: Put them at ease
- **Verify understanding**: "So what you're saying is...?"
- **Follow the process**: Talk to multiple people in the workflow

**Sample Interview Questions**:
- "Can you walk me through how you do [task]?"
- "What do you do if [problem] occurs?"
- "How do you know what the requirements are?"
- "How is this measured/checked?"
- "What training did you receive?"
- "Where do you find [document/information]?"

**What to Avoid**:
- Leading questions: "You do X, right?"
- Yes/No questions only
- Judgmental tone
- Technical jargon (if auditee won't understand)

#### D. Records Sampling

**Purpose**: Verify records are maintained and meet requirements

**Sampling Approach**:
- Random selection (not just best examples)
- Sufficient sample size (risk-based: 5-20 records typical)
- Recent and historical records
- Different shifts/personnel

**What to Check**:
- Completeness (all fields filled)
- Accuracy (data makes sense)
- Timeliness (done when required)
- Authorization (signed/approved as needed)
- Traceability (can track back to source)

#### E. Digital System Checks

**ISO 9001:2026 Enhanced**: Verify digital systems and data integrity

**What to Audit**:
- Access controls (who can view/edit)
- Data validation rules
- Audit trails (change logs)
- Backup and recovery
- Integration between systems
- Data analytics usage
- Cybersecurity controls
- Cloud system reliability

### Evidence Collection Best Practices

**Characteristics of Good Audit Evidence**:
- **Sufficient**: Enough to support findings
- **Reliable**: From credible sources
- **Relevant**: Pertains to audit criteria
- **Verifiable**: Can be confirmed

**Documentation Methods**:
- Take notes (digital or paper)
- Take photos (with permission)
- Collect document references/numbers
- Record interviews (with permission)
- Screen captures (for digital systems)

**Objectivity**:
- Base conclusions on evidence, not assumptions
- Consider multiple sources
- Verify critical findings with additional evidence

### Identifying Findings

#### Finding Types

**1. NONCONFORMITY (NC)**

Failure to meet a requirement

**Major Nonconformity**:
- Complete absence of a required system element
- Systemic failure (affects multiple areas)
- Could result in unsafe product/service
- Likely to result in customer dissatisfaction
- Regulatory violation

Examples:
- No risk assessment process (Clause 6.1)
- No internal audits conducted (Clause 9.2)
- Untrained personnel performing critical tasks (Clause 7.2)

**Minor Nonconformity**:
- Isolated incident
- Unlikely to result in product/service failure
- System exists but not fully effective

Examples:
- One training record missing signature
- Single measurement record incomplete
- Procedure followed but not latest version

**2. OBSERVATION (OBS)**

Not a nonconformity, but notable:
- Potential improvement area
- Good practice to share
- Trend that could lead to NC
- Risk that should be considered

Examples:
- "Consider automating this manual data entry to reduce errors"
- "Three similar customer complaints suggest a trend to investigate"
- "Good practice: Digital dashboard for real-time KPI visibility"

**3. CONFORMITY**

Evidence that requirements are being met
- Should also be documented
- Recognize good practices
- Build confidence in the QMS

### Finding Documentation

**Each finding should include**:

```
FINDING TEMPLATE

Finding Number: [IA-XXX-NC-01]
Type: [Major NC / Minor NC / Observation]
Process/Area: [Process name]
ISO Clause: [X.X.X]
Requirement: [State the requirement]

Evidence:
• [Specific evidence: what was seen, document reference, interview, etc.]
• [Location, date, time]
• [Personnel involved]

Nonconformity Statement (for NCs):
[Clear description of how evidence shows requirement not met]

Example:
"Clause 7.2 requires determination and provision of necessary competence. 
Evidence: Employee X was operating production equipment without required 
training. Training matrix shows training scheduled but not completed. 
Supervisor confirmed employee started 3 weeks ago. This demonstrates 
competence requirements not met before assigning work."

Observation Statement (for OBS):
[Description of potential improvement or good practice]

Auditee Comments:
[Auditee's response/acknowledgment]

Auditor: [Name]  Date: [Date]
```

## Audit Reporting

### Closing Meeting

**Attendees**: Same as opening meeting (or wider)

**Agenda**:
1. Thank participants for cooperation
2. Restate audit scope and objectives
3. Present findings:
   - Strengths and good practices
   - Nonconformities (major first, then minor)
   - Observations
4. Discuss each finding with evidence
5. Clarify any questions
6. Explain next steps (response timeline, follow-up)
7. Opportunity for auditee comments

**Lead Auditor Reminds**:
- Findings are based on sampling
- Additional findings may be identified during report writing
- Corrective action required for NCs
- Follow-up audit may be needed

### Audit Report

**Report Timeline**: Issued within 5 business days of closing meeting

### Audit Conclusion Types

| Conclusion | Criteria |
|------------|----------|
| **Conforming** | No major NCs, few or no minor NCs, process meeting objectives |
| **Nonconforming** | One or more major NCs, or multiple systemic minor NCs |
| **Needs Improvement** | No major NCs, but several minor NCs or significant observations |

### Verification Methods

**Desktop Review**:
- Review submitted evidence documents
- Suitable for minor NCs with clear objective evidence
- Faster and less resource-intensive

**Follow-up Audit**:
- On-site/remote verification visit
- Re-audit the specific area
- Required for major NCs
- Recommended for systemic issues

### Closure Criteria

An NC can be closed when:
1. ✅ Immediate correction completed
2. ✅ Root cause identified
3. ✅ Corrective actions implemented
4. ✅ Evidence of implementation provided
5. ✅ Effectiveness verified (NC has not recurred)

**ISO 9001:2026 Recognition**: Remote auditing is accepted methodology

**Best Practices**:
- Test technology beforehand
- Ensure good camera angles for observations
- Record sessions (with permission)
- Have backup communication method

#### 3. Data Analytics
- Analyze QMS data directly
- Identify trends and anomalies
- Sampling based on data patterns
- Predictive risk indicators

**Examples**:
- Query nonconformity database for trends
- Analyze production data for quality patterns
- Review system logs for access/changes
- Dashboard review for KPI performance

#### 4. AI-Assisted Auditing
- Automated document review
- Natural language processing for records
- Anomaly detection in large datasets
- Risk scoring algorithms

**Caution**: Human judgment still required; AI is an assistant tool

### Digital Evidence Management

**Digital Evidence Types**:
- Screenshots
- System logs and audit trails
- Emails and communications
- Digital records (PDF, database entries)
- Photos and videos
- Metadata (timestamps, authors)

**Best Practices**:
- Timestamp and source all evidence
- Ensure integrity (no tampering)
- Obtain permission for screenshots
- Store securely with audit files
- Consider data privacy regulations
