## Purpose

This procedure establishes a systematic approach to managing risks and opportunities that could affect the organization's ability to:
- Achieve QMS objectives
- Enhance customer satisfaction
- Prevent or reduce undesired effects
- Achieve continual improvement
- Ensure organizational resilience

**ISO 9001:2026 Alignment**: This procedure addresses the enhanced risk-based thinking requirements, including digital risks, climate-related risks, and sustainability considerations.

---

## Scope

This procedure applies to:
- Strategic and operational planning (Clause 6.1)
- All QMS processes and activities
- Product/service realization
- Supplier management
- Change management
- Project management
- Digital transformation initiatives
- Climate adaptation and mitigation
- Sustainability programs

---

## Definitions

### Risk
Effect of uncertainty on objectives (can be positive or negative)

### Opportunity
Positive risk; favorable circumstances to achieve intended results

### Risk Appetite
Amount and type of risk the organization is willing to accept

### Risk Tolerance
Acceptable level of variation around objectives

### Inherent Risk
Risk before controls are applied

### Residual Risk
Risk remaining after controls are applied

### Climate Risk
Physical and transition risks related to climate change

### Digital Risk
Risks associated with digital technologies, data, and cybersecurity

---

## Responsibilities

### Top Management
- Establish risk appetite and tolerance levels
- Allocate resources for risk treatment
- Review significant risks in management review
- Champion risk-aware culture

### Risk Manager / Management Representative
- Maintain risk management framework
- Facilitate risk assessments
- Monitor risk register
- Report on risk trends
- Coordinate risk treatment actions

### Process Owners
- Identify risks within their processes
- Implement risk controls
- Monitor control effectiveness
- Report risk changes

### All Employees
- Report identified risks
- Follow risk control measures
- Participate in risk assessments
- Support risk awareness culture

## Risk Assessment Methodology

### Step 1: Establish Context

**Purpose**: Define the scope and criteria for risk assessment

**Activities**:
1. **Define Objectives**: Clarify what you're trying to achieve
2. **Identify Stakeholders**: Who is affected or has influence
3. **Review Context**: Internal/external factors (SWOT, PESTLE)
4. **Set Criteria**: Define risk appetite and tolerance

**Tools**:
- Context analysis template (Clause 4.1, 4.2)
- Stakeholder mapping
- SWOT analysis
- PESTLE analysis

---

### Step 2: Risk Identification

**Purpose**: Identify sources of risk and opportunities

**Techniques**:

#### 2.1 Brainstorming Sessions
- Cross-functional team workshops
- What could go wrong? What could go better?
- No ideas filtered initially

#### 2.2 Process Analysis
- Review process maps and flowcharts
- Identify failure points
- Analyze process inputs/outputs

#### 2.3 Checklist Analysis
- Use industry-specific risk checklists
- Review past incidents and lessons learned
- Consider regulatory and compliance risks

#### 2.4 SWOT Analysis
- **Strengths**: Internal positive factors (opportunities)
- **Weaknesses**: Internal negative factors (risks)
- **Opportunities**: External positive factors
- **Threats**: External negative factors

#### 2.5 Scenario Analysis
- Best case, worst case, most likely case
- "What if" questioning
- Trend extrapolation

**Risk Categories to Consider**:

| Category | Examples |
|----------|----------|
| **Strategic** | Market changes, competition, reputation |
| **Operational** | Process failure, equipment breakdown, human error |
| **Financial** | Cash flow, currency, credit |
| **Compliance** | Regulatory changes, legal requirements |
| **People** | Competency gaps, turnover, safety |
| **Technology** | System failure, cybersecurity, obsolescence |
| **Supply Chain** | Supplier failure, logistics, quality issues |
| **Customer** | Satisfaction, requirements changes, complaints |
| **Environmental** | Climate change, natural disasters, pollution |
| **Reputation** | Brand damage, social media, stakeholder trust |

---

### Step 3: Risk Analysis

**Purpose**: Understand the nature, likelihood, and consequences of risks

#### 3.1 Likelihood Assessment

Rate the probability of the risk occurring:

| Level | Rating | Description | Probability |
|-------|--------|-------------|-------------|
| **Very Unlikely** | 1 | Rare occurrence | < 10% |
| **Unlikely** | 2 | Could occur occasionally | 10-30% |
| **Possible** | 3 | Might occur | 30-60% |
| **Likely** | 4 | Will probably occur | 60-80% |
| **Very Likely** | 5 | Expected to occur | > 80% |

#### 3.2 Impact Assessment

Rate the consequence if the risk occurs:

| Level | Rating | Impact on Objectives | Financial | Customer | Operations |
|-------|--------|---------------------|-----------|----------|------------|
| **Insignificant** | 1 | Minimal effect | < $10K | No impact | < 1 day disruption |
| **Minor** | 2 | Small effect | $10K-50K | Minor complaints | 1-3 days disruption |
| **Moderate** | 3 | Noticeable effect | $50K-250K | Some dissatisfaction | 1 week disruption |
| **Major** | 4 | Significant effect | $250K-1M | Major complaints | 2-4 weeks disruption |
| **Catastrophic** | 5 | Critical effect | > $1M | Loss of customers | > 1 month disruption |

*Note: Adjust financial thresholds based on organization size*

#### 3.3 Risk Level Calculation

**Risk Score = Likelihood × Impact**

### Step 4: Risk Evaluation

**Purpose**: Determine which risks need treatment

#### 4.1 Risk Prioritization

Sort risks by score (highest to lowest)

#### 4.2 Compare Against Risk Criteria

| Risk Level | Score | Action Required | Timing |
|------------|-------|-----------------|--------|
| **EXTREME** | 20-25 | Mandatory treatment, escalate to top management | Immediate |
| **HIGH** | 12-16 | Treatment plan required, management approval | Within 1 month |
| **MEDIUM** | 6-10 | Treatment plan recommended | Within 3 months |
| **LOW** | 1-5 | Monitor, treatment optional | As resources permit |

#### 4.3 Consider Risk Appetite

- Is the risk within acceptable tolerance?
- Does it align with strategic objectives?
- Are resources available for treatment?

---

### Step 5: Risk Treatment

**Purpose**: Select and implement measures to modify risk

#### 5.1 Treatment Options

**For THREATS (Negative Risks)**:

1. **AVOID**
   - Eliminate the source of risk
   - Stop the activity
   - Change the plan
   - Example: Don't enter high-risk market

2. **REDUCE / MITIGATE**
   - Implement controls to lower likelihood or impact
   - Most common approach
   - Example: Add quality checks, training, backups

3. **TRANSFER / SHARE**
   - Shift risk to third party
   - Insurance, outsourcing, contracts
   - Example: Cybersecurity insurance

4. **ACCEPT**
   - Take no action
   - Monitor and review
   - Used when cost of treatment > risk
   - Requires management approval for HIGH/EXTREME risks

**For OPPORTUNITIES (Positive Risks)**:

1. **EXPLOIT**
   - Ensure opportunity is realized
   - Allocate best resources
   - Example: Fast-track high-value project

2. **ENHANCE**
   - Increase likelihood or impact
   - Invest in success factors
   - Example: Additional marketing for successful product

3. **SHARE**
   - Partner to maximize opportunity
   - Joint ventures, alliances
   - Example: Co-develop with customer

4. **ACCEPT**
   - Take advantage if it occurs naturally
   - No active pursuit

#### 5.2 Treatment Plan

For each significant risk, document:

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Risk ID** | Unique identifier |
| **Treatment Option** | Avoid, reduce, transfer, accept |
| **Actions** | Specific steps to be taken |
| **Owner** | Person responsible |
| **Resources** | Budget, people, tools needed |
| **Timeline** | Start and target completion dates |
| **Success Criteria** | How to measure effectiveness |
| **Residual Risk** | Expected risk level after treatment |

#### 5.3 Cost-Benefit Analysis

- Cost of implementing control
- Benefit of risk reduction
- Return on risk investment
- Priority ranking

---

### Step 6: Monitoring and Review

**Purpose**: Track risk changes and control effectiveness

#### 6.1 Key Risk Indicators (KRIs)

Define metrics that signal risk changes:

| Risk Type | Example KRI |
|-----------|-------------|
| Quality defects | Defect rate trend |
| Customer satisfaction | NPS score, complaint rate |
| Supplier performance | On-time delivery % |
| Cybersecurity | Failed login attempts |
| Financial | Cash flow, receivables aging |
| Climate | Carbon emissions, energy use |

#### 6.2 Review Frequency

| Risk Level | Review Frequency |
|------------|------------------|
| EXTREME | Weekly / Continuous monitoring |
| HIGH | Monthly |
| MEDIUM | Quarterly |
| LOW | Annually |

#### 6.3 Risk Register Updates

Update when:
- New risks identified
- Risk levels change
- Treatment actions completed
- Controls proven effective/ineffective
- Context changes (new regulations, market shifts)

#### 6.4 Trigger Events for Re-assessment

- Audit findings
- Incidents or near-misses
- Customer complaints
- Regulatory changes
- Strategic changes
- Technology changes
- Significant process changes
- Mergers/acquisitions
- New products/services

---

### Step 7: Communication and Reporting

#### 7.1 Risk Reporting

**Monthly Risk Report** (to management):
- Top 10 risks
- Risk trend (increasing/decreasing)
- Treatment actions status
- New/closed risks
- KRI dashboard

**Quarterly Management Review** (to top management):
- Strategic risk profile
- Risk appetite vs. actual exposure
- Major incidents and lessons learned
- Resource needs for treatment
- Emerging risks

**Annual Risk Report**:
- Comprehensive risk landscape
- Risk management effectiveness
- Continuous improvement actions

#### 7.2 Communication Channels

- Risk register (accessible to relevant personnel)
- Digital dashboards (real-time)
- Team meetings
- Management review meetings
- Internal audit reports
- Intranet / knowledge base

## Climate and Sustainability Risks

**ISO 9001:2026 Enhanced Requirement**: Organizations must consider climate-related risks and opportunities.

### Climate Risk Categories

#### Physical Risks
- **Acute**: Extreme weather events (floods, storms, wildfires)
- **Chronic**: Long-term changes (sea level rise, temperature changes, water stress)

**Assessment Questions**:
- Are our facilities in climate-vulnerable locations?
- How does extreme weather affect operations?
- Are supply chains resilient to climate events?
- What is our water and energy dependency?

#### Transition Risks
- **Policy/Legal**: Carbon pricing, emissions regulations
- **Technology**: Shift to low-carbon alternatives
- **Market**: Changing customer preferences
- **Reputation**: Stakeholder perceptions of sustainability

**Assessment Questions**:
- How will carbon regulations affect costs?
- Are products/services climate-aligned?
- Is the supply chain sustainable?
- What are stakeholder ESG expectations?

### Climate Risk Assessment Process

1. **Identify Exposure**: Which assets, operations, supply chains are exposed?
2. **Assess Scenarios**: Use climate scenarios (1.5°C, 2°C, 4°C warming)
3. **Evaluate Impact**: Financial, operational, reputational
4. **Plan Adaptation**: Resilience measures, mitigation actions
5. **Monitor**: Track climate metrics, regulations, science

### Sustainability Risk and Opportunity Integration

**ESG Risk Areas**:

| Factor | Risk Examples | Opportunity Examples |
|--------|---------------|---------------------|
| **Environmental** | Resource scarcity, pollution, waste | Circular economy, renewable energy |
| **Social** | Labor rights, diversity, community impact | Employee wellbeing, social innovation |
| **Governance** | Ethics, transparency, compliance | Ethical leadership, stakeholder trust |

**Treatment Actions**:
- Set science-based targets
- Implement circular economy principles
- Ensure ethical supply chains
- Report transparently (e.g., TCFD, GRI)
- Engage stakeholders

## Digital Risk Management

**ISO 9001:2026 Enhanced Requirement**: Address risks from digital transformation and data management.

### Digital Risk Categories

#### 1. Cybersecurity Risks
- Data breaches
- Ransomware attacks
- System intrusions
- Insider threats

**Controls**:
- Multi-factor authentication
- Encryption
- Security training
- Incident response plan
- Regular security assessments

#### 2. Data Privacy Risks
- Regulatory non-compliance (GDPR, CCPA)
- Unauthorized data access
- Data loss

**Controls**:
- Privacy by design
- Data classification
- Access controls
- Data retention policies
- Privacy impact assessments

#### 3. Technology Risks
- System failures
- Cloud service outages
- Software vulnerabilities
- Legacy system obsolescence

**Controls**:
- Redundancy and backup
- Patch management
- Vendor due diligence
- Business continuity planning
- Technology roadmap

#### 4. AI and Automation Risks
- Algorithm bias
- Automated decision errors
- Job displacement
- Accountability gaps

**Controls**:
- AI ethics framework
- Human oversight
- Explainability requirements
- Change management
- Continuous monitoring

#### 5. Data Quality Risks
- Inaccurate data
- Incomplete data
- Data integrity issues

**Controls**:
- Data governance
- Validation rules
- Data quality metrics
- Master data management

### Digital Risk Assessment

Use standard risk assessment methodology (Steps 1-7) with digital-specific considerations:

- **Likelihood**: Consider threat landscape, attack surface, vulnerability exposure
- **Impact**: Data loss, operational disruption, regulatory fines, reputation damage
- **Treatment**: Technical controls, policies, training, insurance

## Documentation and Records

### Risk Register

**Minimum Contents**:

| Field | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Risk ID | Unique identifier (R-001, R-002, etc.) |
| Date Identified | When risk was first identified |
| Category | Strategic, operational, financial, etc. |
| Description | Clear description of the risk |
| Potential Cause | What could trigger this risk |
| Potential Consequence | Impact if risk occurs |
| Process/Area | Where risk applies |
| Owner | Person responsible for managing risk |
| Inherent Likelihood | Before controls (1-5) |
| Inherent Impact | Before controls (1-5) |
| Inherent Risk Level | L×I score and rating |
| Existing Controls | Current measures in place |
| Control Effectiveness | How well controls work |
| Residual Likelihood | After controls (1-5) |
| Residual Impact | After controls (1-5) |
| Residual Risk Level | L×I score and rating |
| Treatment Decision | Avoid, reduce, transfer, accept |
| Treatment Actions | Specific actions planned |
| Action Owner | Who will implement |
| Target Date | When actions due |
| Status | Not started, in progress, complete |
| Review Date | Next review date |
| KRIs | Monitoring metrics |

### Risk Treatment Plans

For significant risks requiring treatment, document:
- Detailed action steps
- Resource requirements
- Success metrics
- Progress tracking

### Risk Assessment Reports

Document outcomes of risk assessment sessions:
- Date and participants
- Scope and context
- Risks identified
- Prioritization results
- Treatment decisions
- Next steps

### Records Retention

- **Risk Register**: Permanent (current version)
- **Historical Risk Registers**: 5 years minimum
- **Risk Assessment Reports**: 3 years minimum
- **Risk Treatment Plans**: Until actions complete + 1 year
- **Risk Monitoring Reports**: 3 years minimum
