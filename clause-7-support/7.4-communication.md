# 7.4 Communication

## Requirement

The organization shall determine the internal and external communications relevant to the quality management system, including:
- On what it will communicate
- When to communicate
- With whom to communicate
- How to communicate
- Who communicates

## Purpose of Communication

Effective communication ensures that:
- Quality information flows to those who need it
- Stakeholders are engaged and informed
- Requirements and changes are understood
- Feedback is received and acted upon
- Transparency and trust are maintained
- Digital channels are leveraged effectively (ISO 9001:2026)
- Climate and sustainability commitments are communicated (ISO 9001:2026)

## Internal Communication

### Communication Types

**Strategic Communication:**
- **What:** Vision, mission, strategy, quality policy, objectives
- **Audience:** All employees
- **When:** Annual, quarterly reviews, major changes
- **Methods:** Town halls, leadership messages, cascading meetings
- **Responsibility:** Top management, senior leadership

**Operational Communication:**
- **What:** Process requirements, work instructions, daily operations
- **Audience:** Process operators, team members
- **When:** Daily, real-time, as needed
- **Methods:** Shift handoffs, huddles, visual management, digital tools
- **Responsibility:** Supervisors, team leaders, process owners

**Performance Communication:**
- **What:** KPIs, metrics, goals, achievements, gaps
- **Audience:** Relevant teams and management
- **When:** Daily, weekly, monthly depending on metric
- **Methods:** Dashboards, scorecards, review meetings, reports
- **Responsibility:** Data owners, managers, management representative

**Change Communication:**
- **What:** Process changes, new requirements, system updates
- **Audience:** Affected personnel
- **When:** Before, during, after change
- **Methods:** Announcements, training, FAQ, change management process
- **Responsibility:** Change owners, management, communication team

**Issue Communication:**
- **What:** Non-conformities, customer complaints, incidents, risks
- **Audience:** Responsible parties, affected stakeholders
- **When:** Immediate, time-critical
- **Methods:** Alerts, email, phone, system notifications, escalation
- **Responsibility:** Quality team, process owners, management

**Recognition Communication:**
- **What:** Achievements, improvements, quality wins, employee recognition
- **Audience:** Organization, teams, individuals
- **When:** Regularly, after achievements
- **Methods:** Announcements, awards, newsletters, celebrations
- **Responsibility:** Leadership, HR, management representative

### Communication Best Practices

**Effective Internal Communication:**

**1. Clarity:**
- Use simple, clear language
- Avoid jargon and acronyms (or explain them)
- Be specific and concrete
- Provide context and examples
- One message at a time for critical info

**2. Timeliness:**
- Communicate at the right time
- Don't delay critical information
- Regular rhythm for routine communications
- Just-in-time for changes
- Advance notice when appropriate

**3. Relevance:**
- Target the right audience
- Tailor message to audience needs
- Explain "what's in it for me"
- Filter unnecessary information
- Provide appropriate level of detail

**4. Two-Way:**
- Enable feedback and questions
- Listen actively and empathetically
- Create psychological safety
- Respond to inputs received
- Encourage open dialogue

**5. Consistency:**
- Align messages across channels
- Consistent from all leaders
- Reinforce key messages
- Avoid contradictions
- Build on previous communications

**6. Accessibility:**
- Available to all employees
- Multiple formats for different needs
- Language translations if needed
- Accommodate disabilities
- Mobile and remote access
- 24/7 availability for critical info

**7. Engagement:**
- Interactive and participatory
- Use storytelling
- Visual and multimedia
- Gamification where appropriate
- Celebrate and recognize
- Make it interesting and memorable

## Communication Procedures

### Procedure: Customer Communication

**1. Incoming Customer Communication:**
- Receive inquiry/request/complaint via designated channels
- Log in CRM or quality system
- Acknowledge receipt within [X hours]
- Route to appropriate person/department
- Set response expectations
- Track to completion

**2. Responding to Customers:**
- Review customer request thoroughly
- Gather necessary information
- Prepare clear, accurate response
- Review for quality and completeness
- Respond within committed timeframe
- Follow up to ensure satisfaction
- Document communication

**3. Proactive Customer Communication:**
- Schedule: Regular updates, performance reviews
- Changes: Notify before implementation
- Issues: Communicate immediately, transparently
- Successes: Share improvements and achievements
- Feedback: Request input and satisfaction data
- Relationship: Maintain regular contact

### Procedure: Change Communication

**Before Change:**
- Identify stakeholders affected
- Develop communication plan
- Create clear change message (what, why, when, how, who)
- Prepare training materials if needed
- Schedule communication activities
- Set up feedback mechanisms

**During Change:**
- Execute communication plan
- Provide support and resources
- Answer questions and address concerns
- Monitor understanding and adoption
- Adjust communication as needed
- Maintain frequent updates

**After Change:**
- Communicate results and benefits
- Recognize contributions
- Share lessons learned
- Address ongoing issues
- Transition to steady-state communication
- Review communication effectiveness

### Procedure: Crisis Communication

**Preparation:**
- Define crisis scenarios
- Identify crisis team and roles
- Establish communication protocols
- Prepare communication templates
- Set up emergency contact systems
- Train spokespersons

**During Crisis:**
- Activate crisis communication plan
- Assess situation and impact
- Communicate internally first (employees, then stakeholders)
- Provide factual, timely updates
- Show empathy and responsibility
- Correct misinformation quickly
- Maintain communication until resolution

**After Crisis:**
- Communicate resolution and lessons
- Thank stakeholders for support
- Return to normal operations communication
- Review crisis communication effectiveness
- Update crisis plans based on learning
- Rebuild trust and reputation

## Best Practices

### Communication Excellence

**1. Know Your Audience:**
- Understand needs and preferences
- Segment and personalize
- Consider cultural differences
- Accommodate different learning styles

**2. Choose the Right Channel:**
- Match channel to message urgency and complexity
- Use multiple channels for critical messages
- Leverage digital for efficiency (ISO 9001:2026)
- Maintain human touch when needed

**3. Keep It Simple:**
- Clear and concise messaging
- Plain language
- Visual communication
- Structured and organized

**4. Be Transparent:**
- Honest and authentic
- Share both good news and challenges
- Admit mistakes and learning
- Open about limitations

**5. Enable Feedback:**
- Create safe space for input
- Make it easy to provide feedback
- Respond to feedback received
- Show how feedback is used

**6. Measure and Improve:**
- Track communication effectiveness
- Learn what works and what doesn't
- Continuously improve approach
- Benchmark best practices

**7. Leverage Technology Wisely:**
- Use digital tools strategically
- Ensure accessibility and usability
- Protect privacy and security
- Balance automation with human connection

**8. Lead by Example:**
- Leadership models good communication
- Consistency from all levels
- Walk the talk
- Visible and accessible leaders

## Common Challenges and Solutions

| Challenge | Solution |
|-----------|----------|
| Information overload | Prioritize; segment; use pull vs. push; filter relevance |
| Message not received | Multiple channels; confirmation; follow-up; accessibility |
| Message not understood | Simple language; visuals; examples; Q&A; feedback |
| Low engagement | Make relevant; interactive; two-way; recognition; gamification |
| Communication silos | Cross-functional meetings; shared platforms; transparency |
| Remote workforce | Digital tools; video; mobile access; frequent touchpoints |
| Resistance to digital | Training; show value; provide support; gradual adoption |
| Cultural/language barriers | Translation; cultural sensitivity; inclusive approach; visuals |
| Feedback not acted upon | Close the loop; show impact; transparency; accountability |
