# 10.2 Nonconformity and Corrective Action

## 10.2.1 Reaction to Nonconformity

### Requirement

When a nonconformity occurs, including any arising from complaints, the organization shall:
- React to the nonconformity and, as applicable:
  - Take action to control and correct it
  - Deal with the consequences
- Evaluate the need for action to eliminate the root cause(s)

### Understanding Nonconformity

**Nonconformity:** Failure to meet a requirement

**Types of Requirements:**
- Customer requirements (explicit or implicit)
- Statutory and regulatory requirements
- Organizational requirements (policies, procedures, specifications)
- ISO 9001:2026 standard requirements
- Environmental and sustainability requirements (ISO 9001:2026)
- Digital and cybersecurity requirements (ISO 9001:2026)

**Sources of Nonconformities:**
- Internal detection (inspection, testing, monitoring)
- Customer complaints
- Internal audits
- External audits (certification, customer, regulatory)
- Supplier nonconformities
- Management review
- Process monitoring and analysis
- Digital system alerts (ISO 9001:2026)

### Immediate Reaction Steps

**Step 1: Contain (Control)**
- Stop further processing if necessary
- Segregate nonconforming items
- Identify and tag nonconforming products/services
- Prevent unintended use or delivery
- Quarantine if necessary
- Immediate digital notifications (ISO 9001:2026)

**Step 2: Correct (Fix)**
- Rework or repair nonconforming product
- Re-perform nonconforming service
- Sort to identify conforming units
- Adjust process to current production
- Implement temporary fix
- Automated correction where possible (ISO 9001:2026)

**Step 3: Address Consequences**
- Notify affected customers
- Recall or field action if needed
- Provide replacement or refund
- Address safety or compliance issues
- Mitigate environmental impact
- Manage reputational impact
- Transparent stakeholder communication (ISO 9001:2026)

### Disposition of Nonconforming Output

**Options:**
1. **Correction:** Rework, repair, adjustment
2. **Concession:** Accept as-is (with or without customer approval)
3. **Containment:** Downgrade, alternate use, restricted use
4. **Return:** To supplier if externally provided
5. **Scrap/Recycle:** If cannot be corrected economically (ISO 9001:2026)
6. **Reject:** Refuse delivery or return to customer

**Authorization:**
- Define who can authorize each disposition
- Customer authorization when required
- Document authorization
- Verify corrected product before release
- Digital approval workflows (ISO 9001:2026)

## 10.2.2 Root Cause Analysis and Corrective Action

### Evaluate Need for Action

**When is Corrective Action Needed?**
- Recurring or systematic problem
- Significant impact (quality, cost, customer, compliance)
- High risk of recurrence
- Trend indicating deterioration
- Customer or regulatory requirement
- Major audit finding
- Climate or environmental incident (ISO 9001:2026)

**When Correction May Be Sufficient:**
- Isolated, one-time occurrence
- Low impact and low risk
- Root cause is obvious and immediately fixed
- Error-proof solution implemented
- No pattern of similar issues

### Root Cause Analysis

**Purpose:**
Identify the fundamental reason(s) why the nonconformity occurred, not just symptoms

**Root Cause Analysis Methods:**

**1. 5 Whys**
- Ask "why" repeatedly (typically 5 times)
- Simple and quick
- Effective for straightforward problems
- Can be superficial if not thorough

**Example:**
```
Problem: Customer received defective product
Why? Inspector missed the defect
Why? Lighting was poor in inspection area
Why? Light bulb burned out
Why? No preventive maintenance schedule for lighting
Why? Maintenance system doesn't include facility lighting
Root Cause: Lighting not included in maintenance system
```

**2. Fishbone (Ishikawa) Diagram**
- Identify potential causes across categories
- Categories: People, Process, Materials, Equipment, Environment, Methods
- Visual brainstorming tool
- Comprehensive approach
- Good for complex problems

**3. Fault Tree Analysis (FTA)**
- Top-down, logical analysis
- Shows combinations of conditions
- Good for safety and reliability issues
- More complex, requires training

**4. Pareto Analysis**
- Identify vital few from trivial many
- 80/20 rule
- Data-driven prioritization
- Focus resources on biggest impact

**5. Barrier Analysis**
- Identify failed or missing barriers
- Prevention and detection barriers
- Good for safety and quality escapes

**6. Change Analysis**
- Compare current to previous (good) state
- Identify what changed
- Effective when problem is new
- Quick identification

**7. AI-Powered Analysis (ISO 9001:2026)**
- Machine learning pattern recognition
- Big data correlation analysis
- Predictive root cause identification
- Natural language processing of text data
- Computer vision for defect analysis

### Root Cause Analysis Process

**Step 1: Define the Problem**
- What happened?
- When did it happen?
- Where did it happen?
- How many times?
- What is the impact?
- What are the symptoms vs. root cause?

**Step 2: Gather Data**
- Collect facts and evidence
- Review records and data
- Interview people involved
- Observe the process
- Digital data mining (ISO 9001:2026)

**Step 3: Identify Possible Causes**
- Brainstorm potential causes
- Use appropriate RCA tool
- Consider all factors (people, process, materials, equipment, environment, management)
- Include system and culture factors (ISO 9001:2026)

**Step 4: Verify Root Cause(s)**
- Test hypothesis
- Confirm with data or evidence
- Ask: If we eliminate this cause, will the problem go away?
- Ask: Did this cause directly lead to the problem?
- Validate with statistical analysis (ISO 9001:2026)

**Step 5: Identify Contributing Factors**
- What else contributed?
- What allowed root cause to exist?
- System weaknesses
- Management system gaps

### Develop Corrective Action

**Effective Corrective Actions:**
- Eliminate root cause, not just symptoms
- Prevent recurrence
- Proportional to impact and risk
- Feasible and practical
- Sustainable over time
- Verifiable
- Address system issues, not just specific instance

**Types of Corrective Actions:**

**Process Changes:**
- Modify process steps
- Add controls or checks
- Error-proofing (poka-yoke)
- Automation
- Statistical process control (SPC)
- Digital process monitoring (ISO 9001:2026)

**Training and Competence:**
- Training for personnel
- Competency development
- Certification requirements
- Refresher training
- Digital learning modules (ISO 9001:2026)

**Design Changes:**
- Product or service redesign
- Simplification
- Specification changes
- Material substitution
- Sustainable design improvements (ISO 9001:2026)

**Equipment and Infrastructure:**
- Equipment modification or replacement
- Preventive maintenance
- Calibration improvements
- Technology upgrades
- Digital infrastructure (ISO 9001:2026)

**Documentation and Communication:**
- Procedure updates
- Work instruction revisions
- Communication improvements
- Visual management
- Digital work instructions (ISO 9001:2026)

**Management System:**
- Policy changes
- Organization structure
- Resource allocation
- Culture and behavioral changes
- Digital system enhancements (ISO 9001:2026)

### Corrective Action Planning

**Action Plan Elements:**
1. **Root Cause(s) Identified:** What is the root cause?
2. **Corrective Action:** What will be done to eliminate root cause?
3. **Responsible Person:** Who will implement?
4. **Target Date:** When will it be completed?
5. **Resources Needed:** What resources are required?
6. **Verification Method:** How will effectiveness be verified?
7. **Success Criteria:** How will we know it worked?

**Implementation:**
- Assign clear responsibility
- Provide necessary resources
- Set realistic timelines
- Communicate actions
- Document implementation
- Track progress
- Digital project management (ISO 9001:2026)

## 10.2.3 Effectiveness Evaluation

### Requirement

Review the effectiveness of any corrective action taken.

### When to Verify Effectiveness

**Short-term (Immediate):**
- Confirm action implemented
- Check if issue resolved in short term
- Verify no immediate recurrence

**Medium-term (30-90 days):**
- Monitor for recurrence
- Check if root cause eliminated
- Verify process stability
- Real-time monitoring (ISO 9001:2026)

**Long-term (3-12 months):**
- Confirm sustained effectiveness
- Verify no unintended consequences
- Check for related improvements
- Trend analysis

### Verification Methods

**Direct Verification:**
- Re-audit the process
- Re-inspect products/services
- Test the corrected process
- Observe operations
- Customer feedback
- Digital verification (ISO 9001:2026)

**Data Analysis:**
- Monitor key metrics
- Statistical analysis
- Trend charts
- Before and after comparison
- Process capability studies
- Predictive analytics (ISO 9001:2026)

**Indirect Verification:**
- Absence of recurrence
- Improved performance metrics
- Reduced complaints
- Audit findings
- Customer satisfaction
- AI-powered pattern analysis (ISO 9001:2026)

### Effectiveness Criteria

**Successful Corrective Action:**
- ☑ Problem has not recurred
- ☑ Root cause eliminated
- ☑ Process performing within control limits
- ☑ Metrics improved or stable
- ☑ No unintended negative consequences
- ☑ Solution sustainable over time
- ☑ Cost-effective
- ☑ Stakeholders satisfied

**Ineffective Corrective Action:**
- ☒ Problem recurred
- ☒ Root cause still present
- ☒ Metrics unchanged or worse
- ☒ New problems created
- ☒ Solution not sustainable
- ☒ Workarounds still needed

**If Ineffective:**
1. Determine why action was ineffective
2. Was root cause correctly identified?
3. Was corrective action appropriate?
4. Was action fully implemented?
5. Are there other root causes?
6. Develop additional or alternative corrective action
7. Implement and re-verify

**Management System Updates:**

### Change Management

## Corrective Action Process Flow

```
Nonconformity Identified
        ↓
Immediate Reaction (Control, Correct, Consequences)
        ↓
Evaluate Need for Corrective Action
        ↓
    Yes ← → No (Close with Correction Only)
        ↓
Root Cause Analysis
        ↓
Develop Corrective Action Plan
        ↓
Implement Corrective Action
        ↓
Verify Effectiveness
        ↓
Effective? → Yes → Close
        ↓
        No
        ↓
Review and Revise Action → Return to RCA or Corrective Action
```

## Best Practices

### Corrective Action Excellence

**1. Timely Response:**
- React immediately
- Set clear timelines
- Track and escalate overdue
- Prioritize by risk and impact
- Real-time alerts (ISO 9001:2026)

**2. Thorough Root Cause Analysis:**
- Don't rush to solution
- Use appropriate tools
- Involve right people
- Data and evidence-based
- Challenge assumptions
- AI-assisted analysis (ISO 9001:2026)

**3. Effective Actions:**
- Address root cause, not symptoms
- Proportional to impact
- Sustainable solutions
- Error-proof where possible
- System-level thinking

**4. Verify Effectiveness:**
- Don't assume it worked
- Multiple verification methods
- Allow sufficient time
- Monitor long-term
- Close the loop

**5. Learn and Share:**
- Capture lessons learned
- Share across organization
- Update knowledge base
- Prevent similar issues elsewhere
- Continuous improvement mindset

**6. Culture of Openness:**
- No-blame environment
- Focus on system, not people
- Encourage reporting
- Learn from mistakes
- Psychological safety

**7. Leverage Technology:**
- Digital CA systems
- Data analytics
- AI-assisted RCA
- Automated workflows
- Real-time tracking

## Common Challenges and Solutions

| Challenge | Solution |
|-----------|----------|
| Recurring nonconformities | Deep root cause analysis; system-level thinking; verify CA effectiveness |
| Superficial root cause analysis | Training in RCA methods; facilitation; sufficient time allocated; challenge thinking |
| Weak corrective actions | Address root cause not symptoms; error-proofing; pilot test solutions |
| Poor follow-through | Clear ownership; tracking system; management accountability; escalation |
| Defensive culture | No-blame approach; focus on system; psychological safety; leadership modeling |
| Lack of effectiveness verification | Standard verification process; sufficient time allowed; multiple methods |
| Not learning from issues | Lessons learned process; knowledge sharing; similar process reviews |
| Data quality issues | Improve data collection; digital systems; validation; real-time capture |
